### What is Abelian?

Abelian is a Layer 1 blockchain built to keep your money safe and private, even after quantum computers arrive. It runs on its own network with its own coin, ABEL. Every transaction on it has been protected by post-quantum cryptography from the very first block.

<Accordion title="Where does the term, abelian, come from?">
    In math, "Abelian" means commutative: the order of an operation doesn't matter, so a + b = b + a. An Abelian group is a set with an operation like addition where swapping the order always gives the same result. The term is named after the Norwegian mathematician Niels Henrik Abel.
</Accordion>

### Why Abelian Exists

Most blockchains today, including Bitcoin and Ethereum, rely on cryptography that a powerful enough quantum computer could break. If that happens, the keys protecting people's funds and the records of their transactions could be exposed. Some networks plan to upgrade later. Abelian took a different path: it was built quantum-resistant from day one, so there's nothing to migrate and no old cryptography left behind.

Abelian also believes privacy should be a choice, not an afterthought. On a public chain, anyone can follow your balances and payments. Abelian lets you decide how much of each transaction others can see.

### The Technology

Post-quantum security. Abelian is built entirely on lattice-based cryptography. It uses the same lattice (MLWE/MSIS) foundations as NIST's ML-KEM and ML-DSA, the math many experts consider a leading candidate for withstanding quantum attacks. There are no elliptic-curve parts anywhere in the system.

Multi-Layer Privacy. Every time you send ABEL, you choose how private it is:
- Transparent: fast, low-cost transfers that are visible on-chain, much like a regular blockchain.
- Shielded: the sender, the recipient, and the amount are all kept confidential.
- Auditable: privacy toward the public, with the option to share visibility with someone you trust, like an auditor.

Abelian's privacy is quantum-resistant too, so private data recorded today can't simply be decrypted years from now once quantum computers exist.

Proof-of-Work mining. Abelian is secured by miners, not validators. Since the Aconcagua upgrade in January 2026, it has used a hybrid Proof-of-Work that lets both GPUs and CPU or ASIC-style hardware mine, which opens the network to a wider range of participants.

Tokens and smart contracts. The Abelian network supports user-issued tokens (AUT). For smart contracts, there's QDay, an EVM-compatible Layer 2 built for the Abelian ecosystem.

### A Brief History

- 2022: The Abelian whitepaper is published in February, and mainnet goes live in April.
- 2023: Abelian raises a US$3 million round led by Draper Dragon and releases its first desktop wallet.
- 2024: A major network upgrade introduces Multi-Layer Privacy and user-issued tokens.
- 2025: Abelian launches the Desktop Wallet Pro, the QDay Layer 2, and a new issuance schedule designed to spread rewards over roughly six decades.
- 2026: The Aconcagua upgrade brings hybrid mining. In March, a Google Quantum AI research paper names Abelian among the few blockchains that rely exclusively on post-quantum cryptography. In October, Abelian becomes a founding member of the Quantum Alliance, an industry group defining what "quantum-ready" means for blockchains.

### The People Behind Abelian

Abelian was co-founded by Duncan Wong and Huaxiong Wang, both cryptographers with deep academic roots. Duncan Wong, a former professor of computer science at City University of Hong Kong, is one of the original authors of linkable ring signatures. That 2004 research is part of the foundation of private transactions in crypto today. The team has also published peer-reviewed research on lattice-based ring signatures, the technology that powers Abelian's privacy.

<Info>
    In 2004, Abelian co-founder Duncan Wong co-authored the paper that introduced linkable ring signatures, a way to sign for a group without revealing who signed while still catching anyone who tries to spend the same coins twice. That idea later became a building block for Monero's private transactions. [[source: "Linkable Spontaneous Anonymous Group Signature for Ad Hoc Groups"](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/027.pdf)]
</Info>

### What's Next

Abelian is working toward what it calls crypto agility. The upcoming Blanc Fork will let the network support multiple post-quantum signature families side by side, adding Falcon and SPHINCS+ alongside its lattice suite. In Duncan Wong's words, Blanc Fork will make Abelian "the world's first cryptographically agile blockchain." If any single algorithm is ever weakened, the network will be able to switch without users needing to move their funds.

Abelian's mission is to give everyone digital money that stays secure, stays private, and stays theirs, no matter what tomorrow's computers can do. As Duncan Wong puts it, that rests on three pillars: quantum resistance, privacy, and cryptographic agility.
